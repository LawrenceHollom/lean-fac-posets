## Proof changes

- **Chain extension.** `PaperChainExtension` is the quotient in the manuscript. Lean computes with normalized principal and signed constructors, with two auxiliary order endpoints. `normalizeOrderIso` identifies the quotient with the subtype excluding those endpoints. The proof of domination independence uses mutual cofinality and the representative comparison formulas. Finite-trace transfer bounds the trace using its neighboring chain points. Transitivity aligns the three tails and composes the canonical maps, then uses saturation and mutual cofinality to normalize the two outer tails.
- **Scattering and witnesses.** The scattering argument uses a countable dense order built from disjoint lexicographic pairs. The vacillation proof constructs the forbidden sum by taking the principal intervals between consecutive witnesses, then applies the argument to tails to choose the alternating subsequence.
- **Tubes.** The proof still partitions by nonprincipal cuts and applies compactness to the resulting pieces. The finite exhaustion uses the sizes of intervals from a maximal antichain for its two signed directions. The compactness proof uses an ultrafilter on those stages and stabilizes each point’s antichain representative. The finite assignments themselves use height classes and a longest chain.
- **Structural decomposition.** The replacement graph uses stage-tagged points and a well-founded descendant relation. The stable chain is the same eventual-membership set as in the manuscript. The arbitrary-chain upper bound uses the same permanent-removal argument and running maxima.
- **Separating chains.** The open-splitting contradiction uses a maximal set of pairwise separated points and embeds the rationals into its dense order. The paper iterates dyadic intervals. In the bounded integer-chain argument, a finite cover by singleton and incomparable sets and the infinite pigeonhole principle select an entire open chain from one of those sets. The paper tracks both endpoints of each incomparability trace.
- **Consolidation.** The positive sweep and the negative sweep in each gap are constructed as maximal compatible initial portions. This packages the manuscript’s transfinite recursion. Their witnesses are embeddings of signed atomic profiles, using the equivalent ω-ray characterization in vacillating posets. The sweep proof needs FAC and quasifoundedness. Scatteredness is used later to obtain an atomic ray for the maximal-tube proof.
- **Hausdorff induction.** Points are equivalent when every convex subset of the interval between them has the inductive property. Well-ordered cofinal and coinitial slices show that each equivalence class has the same property. Adjacent classes would merge, so the cover property for scattered orders forces the quotient to have at most one point.
- **Consolidation examples.** The first uses a translation of the entire two-lane order to induce both witnesses. The second proves that any improvement of a column germ has a representative with a constant-column tail.
- **Maximal tube.** Finite-trace transfer reduces the contradiction to points of the consolidated chain. The replacement then splices an increasing ray into the convex union of the traces. The proof treats finite and infinite removed intervals separately and checks convexity of the finite-incomparability hull.
